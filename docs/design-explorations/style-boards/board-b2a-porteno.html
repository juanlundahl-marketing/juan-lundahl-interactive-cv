Lo porteño, de todos los días

Rationale. Not the Buenos Aires of postcards: the one you walk past every morning. Enamelled plates screwed to a door frame, the painted fascia of an almacén or a bar notable, the green of a colectivo, gold drop-shadow lettering on a kiosk window, sidewalk tiles under your feet, newsprint cream and a café with medialunas. All of it is redrawn as original geometry and used sparingly (one ornament per section) so the portfolio still reads clean and professional to a recruiter.

Type is Argentine: Sansita and Sansita Swashed (Omnibus-Type) carry the painted-sign voice, Encode Sans Condensed (Impallari Type, Rosario) does colectivo numbers, plates and destination signs, and Archivo (Omnibus-Type) keeps body text calm. Behind the hero, a faint original line drawing of the Sol de Mayo rays, dialled by one variable.

Mobile rules

  • The theatre-bill portrait shrinks to 150px and sits beside a small "Argentina" plate.
  • The Sol de Mayo burst moves to the top-right corner, mostly cropped, behind the portrait only; the headline sits on plain cream.
  • 16px gutter; H1 2.45rem Sansita 900 with the Swashed accent word.
  • Filete corners shrink to 44px and appear only on the hero and featured cards.
  • Pizarra and route panel stack to 2 then 1 column; plates never wrap mid-word.

Cómo se compara con B

What was removed because it read as a visitor's view, and what replaced it.

Out (traveller cues)

  • Transit-ticket "boleto" credential and punch holes.
  • Generic subte line map for markets.
  • The "Buenos Aires" souvenir plate under the portrait.
  • Montserrat caps everywhere: a tidy, international signage look.
  • Pale ink-and-celeste palette that could be any vintage city.

In (everyday porteño)

  • Door nameplate in chipped green enamel, plus the "Abierto / Vuelvo enseguida" shop sign.
  • Colectivo line numbers, destination signs and a route panel with Argentina as cabecera.
  • Shop-fascia work cards, café pizarra stats, baldosa dividers and vereda pads.
  • Painted-sign type (Sansita + Swashed) with gold drop shadow, used for one word at a time.
  • Bondi green, oxblood and ochre added to cream, ink and a weathered celeste; faint Sol de Mayo rays behind the hero.
